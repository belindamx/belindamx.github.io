AI summaries & opinion extremity

Do AI-generated summaries change readers’ opinions?

Paper Code

The experiment

We randomly assigned participants to read Reddit-style discussions with or without an AI summary, then compared how their responses changed.

Survey participants
230
Discussion topics
3
Participant-topic observations
690

Methods

  • Measured opinions and perceived public support before and after reading about AI data centers, police body cameras, and capital punishment.
  • Estimated differences in change with baseline adjustment and topic fixed effects.
  • Clustered standard errors by participant because each person rated three topics. Checked sensitivity to attention and reading time.

R · Randomized experiments · Linear regression

R analysis De-identified data

Results

No clear evidence that summaries reduced opinion extremity.

Adjusted estimates
OutcomeEffect95% CI
Opinion extremity0.006[−0.159, 0.171]
Perceived public support−0.231[−0.456, −0.005]

Summary minus control difference in change. Lower perceived public support appeared only after adjustment, so that finding needs further testing.

Music discovery

Building listening paths between songs and across music communities.

App Paper Code

Methods

  • Connected songs using cosine similarity across seven standardized audio features.
  • Detected communities with Louvain and identified bridge tracks with centrality.
  • Built listening paths with Dijkstra’s algorithm.

Python · Graph algorithms

Results

81,343 songs · approximately 400,000 edges

Bike-share demand

Predicting hourly bike rentals in Seoul from weather, time, and recent demand.

Paper Code

Methods

  • Compared linear regression, a feedforward neural network, and XGBoost.
  • Encoded cyclical time patterns and added lagged demand features.
  • Used a chronological 60/20/20 split to evaluate predictions on later observations. Fitted scaling on the training set only.

Python · XGBoost · TensorFlow

Results

XGBoost · test MAE 96 rentals/hour · R² 0.94

Reported in the project paper
ModelMAE¹R²
Linear regression3270.54
Neural network3260.53
XGBoost (test)960.94

¹ Mean absolute error in hourly rental counts. A test MAE of 96 means predictions differed from actual demand by 96 rentals per hour on average. Reported results; the paper notes some overfitting.
